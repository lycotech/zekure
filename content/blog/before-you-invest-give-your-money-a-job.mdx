Most money mistakes do not start with a bad investment. They start with money that had no job. It sat in an account with no purpose, so when something tempting came along, there was nothing to say no with.

Giving your money a job is simple. It means deciding, before you invest anything, what each naira is meant to do. Here are three questions to answer first.

## 1. What is this money for?

Write it down in one line. "Rent in June." "School fees next term." "A cushion for emergencies." "Something for the long run."

If you cannot say what the money is for, it is not ready to be invested. It is just money waiting for a decision. Start there, because the purpose shapes every choice that follows.

Some goals are close and fixed, like rent or school fees. Others are far away and flexible, like building wealth over many years. They should not be treated the same way.

## 2. When will you need it?

Time is the most useful thing to know about your money. Money you will need in a few months cannot sit comfortably in something that moves up and down a lot, because you may be forced to take it out at a bad moment.

Money you will not touch for many years has more room to ride out the ups and downs. That is why good platforms ask when you will need the money before they show you options, not after.

A simple way to think about it:

- **Needed soon:** keep it somewhere easy to reach, and focus on not losing it.
- **Needed in a few years:** you may be able to accept a little more movement, but you should still know the risks.
- **Needed far in the future:** you have more time, but time does not remove risk.

## 3. What can you afford to lose?

This is the question people skip, and it matters most. Every investment can lose value, and returns are never guaranteed. So before you invest, decide how much you could lose without it hurting your rent, your food, or your family's plans.

If losing a given amount would break your month, that amount should not be invested. Keep your essentials and your emergency fund separate, and only invest what is left over after them.

## Put it together

When you have answered all three questions, you have given your money a job, and you can compare options against it. Read what each option involves, including the fees and the risks, and ask who is providing it and whether they are licensed to do so.

Then take your time. There is no prize for rushing. A plan you understand beats a tip you do not.
